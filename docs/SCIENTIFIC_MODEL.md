# Scientific scope and conceptual model

## Distinct node classes

- **Emotion:** a felt affective state such as joy, sadness, anxiety, anger, regret, grief or calm.
- **Thought:** a verbal, imagistic or propositional mental event.
- **Memory:** a recollection or learned association that may influence present interpretation.
- **Pattern:** a recurring way of attending, interpreting or responding (for example rumination or catastrophizing).
- **Need:** a possible requirement for care, safety, rest, clarity or connection. Needs are prompts for reflection, not fixed diagnoses.
- **Body signal:** a noticed bodily sensation or arousal cue.
- **Behaviour:** an action or tendency to act.
- **Trigger:** an event or cue that may precede a response.
- **Core belief:** a broad assumption about self, other people or the world.
- **Context:** situational conditions such as uncertainty, time pressure or workload.

## Relationship vocabulary

Connections are user-editable hypotheses, such as activates, intensifies, soothes, arises from, recalls, reinforces, inhibits, signals a need, leads to, reframes, supports, conflicts with and co-occurs with. A link records the map author's interpretation; it is not proof of causality.

## Neuroscience caveat

Neurons communicate through complex electrical and chemical processes. Real emotion and cognition emerge from distributed, dynamic systems involving many brain regions and body processes. The app's coloured nodes and directed edges are an explanatory metaphor for reflection and education. They do not correspond one-to-one with neurons, neurotransmitters, brain regions or a person's measured neural activity.

A psychiatric or neurological disorder is not an emotion category. If users choose to add a health-related context node, it should represent their own labelled context and not imply that the application can diagnose or assess a condition.
